Gatlinburg STR cost segregation: cabin additions and the city/county boundary. Review local resources, study records, and a property-specific planning example.

Cabin additions and the city/county boundary

Gatlinburg’s Finance page links overnight-rental procedures and tourist-residency information. Establish whether the parcel is inside the city before using those procedures; a Gatlinburg mailing address is not a substitute for jurisdiction confirmation. Gatlinburg Finance: overnight-rental procedures.

Define the property study

For a cabin with an expanded deck, new hot tub, and access improvements, separate the building project from equipment and site work. Ask for an itemized contractor scope identifying what supports the structure and what serves a separate function. A guest-amenity label does not classify the entire invoice. If the cabin already has a depreciation schedule, reconcile additions to that schedule before commissioning a whole-property estimate.

Records that resolve the main questions

  • City or county jurisdiction confirmation and the parcel identifier
  • Deck drawings, hot-tub purchase records, and electrical scope
  • Original cabin schedule plus dates each later addition became ready for guests

A scoping example

A buyer acquires an operating cabin and adds a second deck before reopening. Prepare separate cost pools for the acquired property, seller furnishings, and new work. Give the preparer the closing inventory and the contractor’s description of the deck foundation; one total remodeling payment cannot explain all three pools.

Illustrative documentation scenario; not a completed client study or a savings estimate.

Does a Sevier County permit file cover my Gatlinburg cabin?

Confirm the actual parcel jurisdiction first. Retain the applicable city or county records as part of the operating file, but use purchase and construction evidence to establish study costs. Permit paperwork and component valuation serve different purposes.
