Accelerating depreciation does not guarantee an immediate reduction in tax. The passive activity rules can limit when a rental loss is deductible. A study should be evaluated with the owner’s actual activity treatment and loss position, rather than an assumed offset against wages.

Map the activity before forecasting the benefit

Rental activities are generally passive, subject to exceptions and special rules. Publication 925 explains the rental exceptions, material participation tests, real estate professional rules and special allowance for certain rental real estate losses. These are separate paths with their own conditions; owning an Airbnb is not itself an exception.

Give the preparer average customer-use periods, service details, owner and manager work, ownership interests and prior grouping decisions. The STR strategy guide connects those facts to the study decision. For long-term rentals or a mixed portfolio, review real estate professional qualification separately.

Track suspended losses rather than assuming a refund

Losses disallowed under the passive activity rules generally carry forward under the applicable rules. That does not establish when they will be usable, or that the owner will receive the same benefit as a currently deductible loss. Keep prior-year carryovers by activity and reconcile them with the current return.

Passive income may allow use of some suspended losses, but activity grouping, ownership and special categories can affect the calculation. Publicly traded partnership rules, for example, require separate attention. Do not treat every loss and every source of passive income as interchangeable.

Other limits can apply as well

A deduction may need review under basis and at-risk rules, followed by passive and other applicable limitations. Nonpassive classification does not eliminate every limit. Ask the preparer for a calculation showing the amount expected to be usable, the amount carried forward and the reason for each restriction.

A sale is not an automatic release of every loss

Publication 925 describes conditions for a disposition of an entire interest in a passive activity in a fully taxable transaction to an unrelated person. Selling one asset, selling to a related party or using a nonrecognition transaction can require different treatment. Grouping can affect what constitutes an entire activity. Do not assume a property sale or 1031 exchange releases all suspended losses against any income.

Connect that review to exchange and basis planning and the recapture guide before relying on exit-year cash flow.

Build a usable-benefit worksheet with the preparer

  1. Identify the activity and applicable participation conclusion.
  2. Reconcile income, current deductions and prior carryovers.
  3. Apply the relevant owner-level limits and state treatment.
  4. Compare the usable timing benefit with study and filing costs.
  5. Document assumptions about future income and disposition rather than treating them as certain.

A later study does not permit an owner to choose any convenient catch-up year without a procedural review. See the Form 3115 guide for that separate decision and when to pause if benefit remains uncertain.
