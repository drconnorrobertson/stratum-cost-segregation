The commonly discussed STR “100-hour test” requires more than 100 hours and participation at least as great as that of any other individual for the year, including nonowners. Exactly 100 hours does not meet that test. This is one material-participation test, not a universal tax qualification rule for Airbnb owners.

Establish the activity treatment first

The average-customer-use exception and the material-participation tests answer separate questions. A short-term rental platform label or local permit does not establish the federal result. Ask the preparer to evaluate the actual rental periods, services, activity and applicable limitations before applying a participation test.

Three illustrations of the comparative test

These fictional examples assume the preparer has established the relevant activity and counted qualifying participation correctly. They test only the two stated conditions, not whether a loss is deductible.

The threshold and the comparison both matter
Assumed factsResult under this test only
Owner 100 hours; every other individual fewerThe owner has not exceeded 100 hours.
Owner 120 hours; highest other individual 120The stated conditions are met; a tie can meet the comparative condition.
Owner 120 hours; manager 160The comparative condition is not met. Ask the preparer whether another test applies.

Use task evidence rather than an hour target

Keep a dated account of actual tasks, the relevant activity, time and supporting references. Distinguish owner work, personal time and investor monitoring for preparer review. A complete-looking spreadsheet is not stronger than an honest record that identifies uncertainty. Do not reconstruct a log simply to reach a target.

Useful supporting references can include maintenance communications, guest-service records, project correspondence and manager statements. These records should describe what happened. The preparer determines whether particular work counts.

Request information about other individuals

An annual management fee does not show how much time each individual spent. Ask the manager to identify roles, available task records and the limits of any time estimates. Avoid treating a management company as one person or adding unrelated work without explaining the activity.

The manager records guide covers property evidence as well as useful operating records. Tell the preparer when cleaner, contractor or manager participation is unknown; an unsupported assumption about someone else’s hours cannot establish the comparison.

A study is a separate workstream

Material participation does not establish the home’s basis, component classifications or bonus eligibility. Give the preparer the ownership and cost information alongside the activity records. Evaluate the usable benefit and the $1-per-square-foot Stratum fee, with a $2,000 minimum per property, before commissioning the study.
