Replacing part of a rental property can raise two separate tax questions: how to account for the old component and how to treat the new work. Paying for a new roof, floor or HVAC system does not automatically establish a deductible loss for the removed component.

Identify what was actually disposed of

The MACRS disposition regulations govern the circumstances in which a component disposition is recognized, including applicable elections and mandatory rules. Begin with the specific property, the component removed, the event and date, and the tax account in which it was depreciated. Insurance, casualty, sale and ordinary replacement facts may require different analysis.

  • Retain dated photographs, contractor scope, removal evidence and invoices identifying the replaced area.
  • Connect the removed component to the original building or improvement schedule.
  • Identify any remaining portion still in service so the same basis is not written off twice.
  • Record the new work separately, including repair-versus-capitalization questions for the preparer.

The new invoice is not the old component’s basis

A loss calculation needs a supported basis for the old component and its depreciation history, not simply the cost of today’s replacement. Regulation 1.168(i)-8 addresses reasonable methods when the disposed portion’s unadjusted depreciable basis is not separately identified. The chosen method must fit the facts and be applied consistently with the rules.

A cost segregation report may provide useful detail, but it is not the only possible evidence and does not itself make the election. Ask the preparer to document the method, the original cost inputs, depreciation allowed or allowable and the resulting adjusted basis.

Raise the election question during the replacement year

The regulation generally ties the partial disposition election to the timely filed original return, including extensions, for the disposition year. Do not assume an old replacement can be claimed whenever a later study is ordered. If the filing deadline has passed, a professional must evaluate whether any applicable relief or other procedure is available. Form 3115 is not a universal cure for a missed election.

Before filing: obtain a written conclusion on the disposed asset, basis method, depreciation adjustment, election requirements and new asset treatment. A contractor invoice alone does not answer those questions.

STR replacements need a property-level record trail

Frequent guest turnover may create recurring furnishing replacements as well as building work. Replacing a sofa already listed as a separate asset calls for review of that asset’s schedule; replacing one component embedded in the building presents a different basis question. Use the STR furnishing records guide and component evidence guide to keep those events distinct.

Coordinate the report and return

Provide the study team with prior disposals before a new report is finalized, and have the preparer reconcile the report to the asset ledger. The report handoff guide helps assign responsibility for the study, elections and return implementation. For a broader existing-property review, start with the property history checklist.
