After fire, flood or other damage, an STR owner needs a coordinated insurance, basis and tax review. A repair bill is not automatically the deductible loss, and insurance proceeds are not automatically tax-free because they will be used to rebuild.

Preserve evidence before it disappears

  • Record the event date, damaged property and business or personal-use facts.
  • Retain photographs, adjuster reports, valuation evidence and contractor scope.
  • Gather the original study and current depreciation schedules, not just the report's first-year summary.
  • Separate property-damage reimbursements from other payments and record amounts received or expected.
  • Track salvage, removed components and replacement costs separately.

The loss calculation depends on the facts

Publication 547 distinguishes damaged property from business or income-producing property that is completely destroyed or stolen. Adjusted basis, value decline where applicable, salvage and reimbursements affect the computation. Do not apply the personal-use rules automatically to a rental, or apply rental rules to every portion of a vacation home with owner use.

A study may help identify components, but it does not guarantee every damaged item has a separate, current adjusted-basis line. Reconcile depreciation allowed or allowable and later improvements. A fully depreciated asset can have little remaining tax basis even when replacement is expensive.

Coordinate casualty and disposition treatment

The MACRS disposition regulations address casualty-related component dispositions. That review must be coordinated with the casualty computation so the same basis is not deducted twice. Do not claim an automatic component write-off plus a separate casualty deduction on that same amount. Use the partial disposition guide to organize the questions; the preparer must determine the treatment.

Reimbursements can produce a gain

Insurance exceeding the relevant adjusted basis can create a gain even if the owner experienced an economic loss. Recapture and possible section 1033 postponement require separate review. Replacement eligibility, amount reinvested, elections and deadlines matter. The four-year disaster replacement rule described for a main home or its contents is not a universal four-year rule for rental property.

Ask the adviser to document the actual replacement period and any relief applicable to the event. Do not assume rebuilding always defers gain or creates a full new cost basis. See the recapture review.

Reconcile the rebuild before a new study

Keep surviving assets, disposed assets and new construction distinct. Deferred gain may affect replacement basis. Classifications and bonus eligibility need their own support rather than an assumption that every reconstruction cost is immediately deductible. The component evidence guide and report handoff help maintain that record trail.
