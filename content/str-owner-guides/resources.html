2. Match the evidence to your property

  • Furnished acquisition: reconcile the seller inventory and later purchases.
  • Renovation: request contractor detail for the actual completed work.
  • Condo: separate unit assets and common-property boundaries.
  • Phased opening: distinguish acquisition, readiness and later projects.
  • Several rentals: keep each property’s costs and history separate.
  • Leased home: identify the landlord’s property and your own investment.

4. Review the tax questions with your preparer

  • Material participation: understand the comparative test and its limits.
  • Furniture and equipment: prepare the records before assigning classifications.
  • Bonus depreciation: review acquisition history, dates and applicable rules.
  • Sale and recapture: retain asset history for the exit decision.

A completed study does not automatically establish a current deduction or a tax reduction. These topics help you prepare focused questions for your own facts.
