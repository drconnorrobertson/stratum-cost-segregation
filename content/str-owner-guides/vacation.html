Cost segregation for a vacation rental should begin with the property’s supported costs, ownership and actual use. A beach condo, mountain cabin or seasonal home is not automatically a good study candidate because it has guest amenities or a high purchase price. Evaluate the evidence and the benefit you can use before commissioning the work.

Personal use belongs in the review from the start

Keep a calendar distinguishing guest rental periods, owner and family use, availability and other relevant events. Publication 527 describes special rules for a dwelling used both as a rental and as a home. Personal use is not a universal all-or-nothing disqualification; the facts and applicable limitations require review.

Give the preparer the actual use records instead of a general statement that the home is mostly rented. Identify partial-property use and periods when a unit or area was unavailable. The property study and the owner’s tax treatment are separate workstreams.

Reconcile what came with the property

For a furnished vacation home, obtain the executed seller inventory, exclusions and closing amendments. Separate later purchases and replacements. An attractive listing can help describe the property, but it does not prove which items transferred, what they cost or whether the owner separately owns a shared amenity.

For a condo or resort arrangement, include the deeded interest, unit boundaries and association records. Review common-area ownership and seller-furnishing reconciliation before sending a combined cost estimate.

Amenities need facts, not automatic classifications

A hot tub, deck, pool, outdoor kitchen or game-room installation may involve several assets and cost scopes. Describe ownership, function, installation and the underlying invoices. Do not assume that premium finishes guarantee a higher short-life allocation or that every outdoor item is fifteen-year property.

The component evidence guide helps frame these questions. The study should explain the classifications and limitations for your property, not target a percentage copied from another home.

Evaluate cost and timing without a savings promise

Stratum pricing is $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Compare the study fee and separately engaged implementation costs with incremental deductions your preparer expects you can use. Include state treatment, future depreciation and an expected sale in the comparison.

Neither an amenity budget nor an assumed marginal tax rate establishes a refund. Use the study economics checklist and the reasons to wait or decline. A study is an optional decision requiring support, not an automatic requirement for every vacation rental.

Use market guides to prepare the right property file

Property records, ownership boundaries and local operating arrangements can vary by location. Use the market guide directory to locate relevant preparation questions. The location name does not determine a federal asset classification or prove that a particular tax benefit is available.

Bring the evidence into discovery

Prepare closing records, size, furnishing inventory, improvement history, rental-use records and prior depreciation. Ask the provider to state the scope, inspection approach, missing-record requirements and delivery dependencies. For broader context, explore the STR study service and owner resources.
