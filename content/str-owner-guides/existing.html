You can evaluate a cost segregation study after a property has entered service. Whether it is useful depends on the available records, prior tax treatment, remaining depreciation and the procedure needed to implement a supported change. A later report does not automatically produce a current catch-up deduction.

Reconstruct the property’s tax history first

Give the study team and preparer a common record set. A closing statement alone will not explain several years of renovations, furnishing purchases and rental-use changes.

  • Original purchase documents, land allocation and basis support.
  • Original and subsequent placed-in-service dates, with evidence of readiness for intended use.
  • Every available depreciation schedule and relevant prior elections or method changes.
  • Improvement invoices, reimbursements and costs already expensed or capitalized.
  • Assets replaced, sold or removed, including furnishings recorded separately.
  • Personal-use periods, rental conversions and ownership or entity changes.

The STR document checklist includes a blank register for tracking missing evidence. Mark an unavailable record as unresolved rather than filling it with an unsupported estimate.

Keep older assets separate from later projects

An Airbnb acquired several years ago may now include furniture bought this year and a renovation completed in stages. Each event needs its own cost and service-date history. Do not assign all items the date of the study, or depreciate an already removed asset as though it remains in service.

See the furnishing guide and replacement review for those separate questions. A later switch between rental types also calls for the change-in-use review.

Historic dates still control the tax review

The preparer must examine the law, elections and other requirements applicable to the assets. A study performed now does not reset the acquisition date or automatically grant current-year bonus treatment to old property. Conversely, do not assume historic bonus treatment is irrelevant simply because the original return has already been filed.

Resolve the implementation route before promising savings

Ask the preparer whether the proposed treatment requires a method change, amended return or another applicable procedure, and whether the taxpayer and assets qualify. A section 481 adjustment, when appropriate, needs a reconciliation of the old and proposed methods. Use the Form 3115 procedural guide for that discussion.

Engagement checkpoint: identify who supplies missing schedules, approves the basis reconciliation, prepares the filing work and updates the asset ledger. Do not assume the study fee includes every tax implementation task.

Compare usable benefit with the full cost

Ask for the expected timing of deductions after applicable loss limits, not just the largest theoretical first-year amount. Include study and filing fees, remaining holding period, state treatment and sale consequences. Longer ownership or a higher property value does not automatically establish a worthwhile result.

The loss limitation review and reasons to wait help identify when more information is needed. When records and economics support proceeding, compare written scope using the proposal checklist.
