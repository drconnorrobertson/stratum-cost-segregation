A cost segregation study begins with a supported depreciable basis. Land is not depreciable. The split between land and other property needs evidence; it is not an election to choose whichever percentage creates the largest deduction.

Allocate a combined purchase using supported values

IRS Publication 551 explains allocating a lump-sum purchase among assets using their relative fair market values at purchase. If land and building values are uncertain, it permits allocation based on assessed values for real estate tax purposes. An assessment ratio is therefore not inherently an unacceptable method. Its date, scope and fit with the property still deserve review.

There is no universal land percentage that fits a lakefront cabin, an urban townhouse and a rural rental. A financing appraisal, acquisition-date valuation and relevant assessment records may supply different evidence. Resolve significant inconsistencies with the preparer rather than selecting the lowest land number.

Build a basis reconciliation before classifying components

  1. Confirm total acquisition cost and the treatment of closing adjustments with the preparer.
  2. Identify land, building, separately transferred furnishings and other assets actually acquired.
  3. Document the value source, valuation date and allocation method.
  4. Reconcile all allocated costs to the approved total.
  5. Record later improvements separately so they are not counted again in the purchase allocation.

A replacement-cost estimate is not automatically the tax basis of an asset. The report should explain how its component estimates connect to the acquisition allocation. See how to review the report reconciliation.

Outdoor property and ownership boundaries

Depreciable site improvements should not be confused with land, but outdoor location alone does not establish a recovery period. Review the land improvement evidence guide. For condos, identify what the owner owns before allocating common amenities or association property.

For a furnished Airbnb purchase

Keep the purchase agreement, seller inventory and any supported furnishing allocation together. A furniture allocation should not duplicate costs already assigned to the building. Later furniture orders need separate records. The STR furnishing guide explains the handoff.

Keep the conclusion through ownership and sale

Retain the source documents and approved allocation with the depreciation schedules. A later study should not silently replace the land number. If evidence indicates a prior allocation needs correction, ask the preparer about the appropriate procedure before changing the ledger. Review existing-property records when reconstructing older files.
