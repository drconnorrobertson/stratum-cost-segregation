Cost segregation is a process for identifying and supporting the portions of a property’s depreciable cost assigned to different asset categories. Some qualifying components may be depreciated over shorter periods than the building. The study changes the timing of supported depreciation; it does not create an additional purchase cost or establish a guaranteed tax saving.

Start with the property’s cost

The analysis needs a supported depreciable basis. Land is not depreciable. Closing costs, later improvements, separately purchased furnishings and other amounts require their own review. The study should reconcile its allocations to the relevant total rather than count an invoice twice.

For example, if a preparer establishes a depreciable cost pool, every allocation in the report must reconcile to that pool. A percentage estimate alone does not establish which components qualify. Current market value and a listing’s advertised amenities do not substitute for the tax basis records.

What the study team examines

  • Acquisition or construction records and the basis reconciliation.
  • Building plans, photographs, inspection evidence and component function.
  • Ownership boundaries, including leased assets and association common property.
  • Costs, installation details and dates the relevant assets were ready and available for use.
  • Existing schedules, improvements and disposals that affect the report.

The IRS examination reference describes study evidence and reconciliation considerations; it is not provider certification or a guarantee that a report will be accepted. Use the report review guide to evaluate the deliverable.

Recovery periods require classification evidence

Five-, seven- and fifteen-year examples are useful orientation, but an asset’s name or expected wear does not determine its tax class. Ownership, function, activity and applicable rules matter. Review the recovery-period examples and outdoor improvement questions before relying on a simple category list.

What changes for an STR or Airbnb owner?

A furnished purchase, separate furniture orders, personal stays, management arrangements and phased renovations create records to reconcile. The study does not decide whether rental losses can offset W-2 income. The preparer must address the activity and loss rules separately.

Start with how studies work for Airbnb owners for a furnished-property example, or the document checklist when preparing a specific property.

How to decide whether to order a study

  1. Confirm ownership, basis and service dates with the preparer.
  2. Ask what supported acceleration could be available under the applicable year’s rules.
  3. Determine whether and when the deductions could be used.
  4. Compare timing benefits with study, filing and exit costs.
  5. Agree on deliverables and implementation responsibility.

Stratum’s study fee is $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Confirm any separately engaged tax advisory or filing costs. There is no universal property-value or income threshold that makes every study worthwhile.
