An Airbnb arbitrage operator leases a property and offers permitted short stays under the applicable agreements. That operating model does not, by itself, give the operator depreciable ownership of the landlord’s building. Before discussing cost segregation, identify the assets and costs the operator actually owns.

Start with three ownership questions

  1. Who owns the building and existing installed components?
  2. Who owns the purchased furnishings and equipment?
  3. Who is treated as owner of each improvement after considering the lease, allowances and reimbursements?

Provide the lease, work letter, amendment history, invoices and reimbursement records together. An expense paid from the operator’s bank account is not, by itself, a complete answer to tax ownership.

A leased building and tenant improvements are different

IRS Publication 946 explains that a lessee generally cannot depreciate the leased property’s cost without the incidents of ownership, but can depreciate qualifying capital improvements it makes. Ask the preparer to establish the treatment of the specific improvement.

Do not automatically use the shorter of useful life and remaining lease term as the federal tax recovery period. Determine the applicable tax classification and rules instead. A lease-end business plan and the tax depreciation schedule answer different questions.

Separate startup purchases from construction work

Create an inventory of movable furnishings, installed work, pre-existing items supplied by the landlord and items provided by a manager. For each invoice, show any credit or reimbursement and the property where the item is used. Keep the approved improvement scope separate from repairs and operating charges for preparer review.

Fictional records example: an operator has a $30,000 furnishing invoice, a $12,000 electrical invoice and a $5,000 landlord reimbursement. Those figures are not automatically one $37,000 depreciable asset. Identify what the reimbursement covers and who owns the resulting work before calculating basis or selecting any treatment. This is an intake illustration, not a deduction calculation.

Does the operator need a study?

A straightforward furnishing schedule may be a different assignment from a study of complex improvements. Ask the preparer what analysis is missing, then request a scope for that assignment. Do not buy a building-wide study solely because the listing earns short-term rental revenue.

Stratum studies cost $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Confirm whether the proposed assignment fits the service and how its fee is determined. Operating expenses, safe-harbor elections and other tax provisions require separate eligibility review; no deduction follows automatically from being an arbitrage operator.

Keep landlord and operator files from overlapping

When both parties have advisers, agree on a factual schedule of work, payment, ownership and reimbursement. Resolve duplicate invoice claims before either party relies on the schedule. Keep original documents and both advisers’ requests accessible through the agreed secure sharing process.

Review furnishing records, the study document checklist and when a study may not fit. An operator considering buying a home should evaluate the acquisition separately rather than assuming ownership guarantees a tax result.
