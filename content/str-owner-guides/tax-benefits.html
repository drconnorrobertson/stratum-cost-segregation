An STR or Airbnb tax review should start with income, expenses, property basis, actual use and the owner’s tax position. Cost segregation is one possible part of that review. It does not create a standard deduction percentage, automatically offset wages or make every operating payment currently deductible.

Build a complete rental-year file

Reconcile platform statements, direct bookings, refunds and manager statements to the books. Keep the underlying invoices for furnishing purchases, improvements and operating charges. Describe personal use, availability and changes in the hosting model. A net platform payout is a payment record, not a complete explanation of the year’s transactions.

Your preparer determines reporting and deductibility. Give them enough detail to distinguish operating costs, prepaid amounts, capital expenditures and personal items instead of assuming every payment belongs in the same category.

Use current bonus-depreciation rules and the right dates

Publication 946 describes restored 100% bonus depreciation for certain qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, subject to the applicable conditions and elections. Older phase-down language should not be applied to every 2026 acquisition. A new study also does not change the original acquisition history of an older property.

Keep separate dates for the house, later furniture and improvements. Ask the preparer which rules apply to each group. Review the current bonus checklist and the historical-date guide where earlier assets are involved.

Evaluate use and participation separately

Customer-use periods, services and participation affect the activity analysis. Personal use and other loss limitations can change the outcome. Do not treat an Airbnb label as proof that the owner can deduct a loss against wages. Review the separate decisions behind the STR strategy with the preparer.

What a cost segregation study contributes

A property study supports the component analysis and the cost reconciliation. It should identify its evidence and limitations. It cannot promise that a standard 20%, 30% or 40% of every purchase qualifies for accelerated treatment. Furnishings, installed work, land and remaining building costs need their own supported treatment.

Use the component evidence guide and the furnishings guide to prepare questions before comparing scopes.

Make a multi-year decision

Compare incremental deductions with the study fee, return implementation, state adjustments and anticipated sale consequences. Stratum pricing is $1 per square foot with a $2,000 minimum per property. Ask how the conclusion changes if a deduction is deferred or you sell sooner than planned. Waiting or declining can be the right decision.

Bring focused questions to your advisers

  • Which source records or use facts are still missing?
  • What is the deduction without a study, and what is incremental?
  • What portion can be used in the modeled period, and why?
  • Which filing, election and state adjustments are required?
  • Who is responsible for the final asset schedule and later changes?
