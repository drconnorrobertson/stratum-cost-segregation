A cost segregation study evaluates supported component costs and classifications. It is not a universal shopping list of items that always qualify for five-, seven- or fifteen-year treatment. For an STR or Airbnb, start with ownership, actual function, installation facts and the evidence behind each cost.

Reconcile the purchase before adding later costs

A furnished acquisition may already include transferred furniture in the allocated purchase pool. Later purchases need separate invoices and dates. Keep land, existing items and improvements distinguishable so the report does not count an asset twice. Follow the seller-inventory workflow before supplying an additional furniture allowance.

Trace the reasoning and the cost

The IRS audit guide discusses classification rationale, documentation and reconciliation. It is an examination reference, not certification of a firm or report. Ask how a material component connects to photographs, plans, invoices or explained costing work, and what remains uncertain.

Where historical records are missing, the provider should explain its method and limitations. Replacing unknown facts with precise-looking numbers does not improve the evidence. Ask the preparer and study team to resolve material differences before implementation.

Classifications do not answer every tax question

Recovery period, depreciation system, bonus eligibility, state treatment and disposition consequences require the appropriate review. A fifteen-year period does not, by itself, establish the asset’s entire sale treatment. Review recovery-period examples and their limits, then plan the later sale review.

A practical STR scope example

A cabin renovation invoice includes a deck, freestanding furniture and equipment connections. Request the factual scopes and cost support for each instead of asking the contractor to label the entire invoice “land improvements.” This is an evidence example, not a conclusion about those assets’ recovery periods.
