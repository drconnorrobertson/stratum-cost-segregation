Compare STR cost segregation proposals by the assignment each firm will perform, the evidence it will use and the work your CPA must do afterward. Send the same factual property file to every provider. Differences in purchase basis, furnishings or timing assumptions can create very different projections before methodology is considered.

Define the assignment in one paragraph

Identify the property and owner, acquisition or construction event, rental use, earlier depreciation and later projects. State whether the quote should cover a building acquisition, a renovation or a limited set of improvements. Ask the firm to list exclusions and unresolved inputs. Keep that assignment with every proposal.

Distinguish three work products

  • Preliminary estimate: assumptions used to discuss possible scope or benefit; it is not the final component analysis.
  • Study report: property-level findings, supporting cost and classification work, schedules and stated limitations.
  • Return implementation: owner-level eligibility, elections, filing procedure, tax software entries and state adjustments determined by the responsible preparer.

Confirm which products are included and who is responsible for the rest. A firm may provide more than one, but a study fee should not be assumed to include every related tax service.

Compare the complete engagement price

Record study fees, travel or rush charges, revisions, support and separately engaged implementation. Stratum charges $1 per square foot with a $2,000 minimum per property. Do not compare that amount with a competitor’s starting price unless the property, product and included work match.

Keep the proposed tax benefit in a separate column from the contract price. Your preparer should evaluate the incremental deduction and its usability before it is described as savings. Use the fee-versus-assumed-benefit worksheet for transparent arithmetic.

Ask for a relevant report example

Request a redacted example suited to your property type. Can you identify the property scope, cost reconciliation, component schedule, methodology and limitations? The IRS audit guide discusses these report considerations, but referencing it is not provider certification. Review the report acceptance guide with your CPA.
