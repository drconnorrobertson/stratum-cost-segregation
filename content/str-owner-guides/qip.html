Qualified improvement property, or QIP, is a specific depreciation category. It is not a general label for any improvement to a rental. An Airbnb renovation does not qualify simply because guests stay briefly or the work is inside the building.

Start with the building classification

Publication 946 describes QIP as certain section 1250 improvements made by the taxpayer to the interior of nonresidential real property, placed in service after 2017 and after the building was first placed in service by any person. The building's tax classification must be established first. Do not assume a short-stay listing automatically makes a house nonresidential real property.

Check the scope and exclusions

Improvements attributable to enlarging the building, elevators or escalators, and the internal structural framework are excluded. Outdoor work is not an interior improvement. Buying an already renovated building does not automatically make the seller's work the purchaser's QIP.

  • Identify who made and owns the improvement.
  • Separate interior work from exterior, expansion and structural scope.
  • Document when the building first entered service and when the new work became ready and available.
  • Reconcile landlord contributions, tenant spending and reimbursements.
  • Retain itemized contracts, plans and invoices supporting the categories.

QIP is not interchangeable with personal property

A project can contain several categories. Equipment, furnishings, building work and qualifying interior improvements need separate analysis; an invoice description is not enough to assign a recovery period. The component evidence guide explains the supporting questions. Preserve ownership distinctions in leased premises rather than allocating every tenant improvement to the landlord.

Recovery period and bonus are separate decisions

QIP placed in service after 2017 is generally 15-year property under GDS, but applicable ADS requirements can change its treatment. For example, an electing real property trade or business under section 163(j) has relevant ADS rules. Bonus depreciation eligibility, historical acquisition facts and elections must be checked separately; a 15-year label alone does not guarantee full expensing.

Section 179 has its own eligible-property and taxpayer requirements. Do not treat it as an automatic substitute if QIP or bonus treatment is unavailable. Review the applicable rules with the preparer before ordering work around a projected deduction.

Connect renovation costs to the old asset schedule

A new improvement does not automatically remove the old component's basis. Review disposition timing and basis separately. For prior returns, the method-change guide explains why a new study is not itself a filed correction.

For an STR project, begin with the document register and request a CPA-ready reconciliation of each cost category, its basis and its service date.
