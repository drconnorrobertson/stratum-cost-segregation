Some depreciable land improvements can have a 15-year recovery period under the applicable depreciation rules. That does not make every outdoor project 15-year property. Land itself is not depreciable, and an outdoor location does not determine an asset’s recovery period.

Classify the work by function, not its invoice label

An STR listing may advertise a pool, patio, deck, driveway or landscaped yard. The tax review needs more detail than the listing. Ownership, construction, use, connection to a building and the applicable asset class matter. A utility line or structural component may require a different conclusion from an independent site improvement.

Owner recordQuestion it helps resolve
Plans and installation photographsWhat was built, where it connects and what it serves
Itemized site-work invoicesWhich costs belong to each asset rather than a combined outdoor budget
Title and association documentsWhether the owner owns the improvement or paid for common property
Completion and use recordsWhen the specific improvement was ready and available for its intended use

Keep land, building and site costs reconciled

A report should explain its cost sources and reconcile allocations to the relevant depreciable basis. Do not move land value into a depreciable category or count the same contractor costs in both a building allocation and a separate site asset. If the original purchase included existing improvements, distinguish allocated acquisition costs from later construction spending.

The component evidence guide explains the support behind a classification. The recovery-period examples are a starting point for questions, not an asset-by-name determination.

Review bonus eligibility separately

A short recovery period alone does not establish the applicable deduction. Acquisition and placed-in-service dates, the depreciation system, elections and other eligibility requirements must be reviewed using the rules for that property. An existing improvement does not receive a new acquisition date merely because a study is completed this year. See the bonus depreciation guide.

Practical questions for Airbnb owners

  • Does a combined outdoor renovation invoice separate the pool, building alterations, furnishings and landscaping?
  • Were projects completed at different times, rather than all ready when the first booking began?
  • Does an association own any of the land or amenities?
  • Did personal use or a later rental conversion change the depreciation review?
  • Has any earlier improvement already been replaced or removed?

Plan for later replacements and sales

Do not assume every land improvement has the same recapture treatment or sale tax rate. Maintain its classification and depreciation history through the disposition review. For replacements, the partial disposition guide explains why old basis and election timing matter. For previously filed returns, consult the method-change review before assuming a current catch-up deduction.
