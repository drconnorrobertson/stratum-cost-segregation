Changing an STR to a long-term rental can require a review of depreciation, activity treatment and existing loss records. It does not automatically mean every nightly rental has a 39-year building life that becomes 27.5 years on conversion. Establish the actual use and applicable classifications before changing the schedule.

Keep the physical asset and the rental activity separate

The building’s depreciation classification and the passive-activity treatment of the owner’s operation are different questions. A booking length used in one analysis does not settle the other. Give your preparer the prior classification, the facts supporting it and the new operating arrangement.

Prepare a before-and-after file

  • Last short-stay reservations, availability history and the new lease or management agreement.
  • Any personal-use periods or periods when the property was unavailable.
  • Prior depreciation schedules, study report and election history.
  • Furnishings sold, retained, moved or newly purchased.
  • Projects completed during the transition and their source records.
  • Existing loss carryforwards and the preparer’s activity records.

Document the transition as it happened. Choosing January 1 as a planning date does not, by itself, eliminate every classification or reporting question.

Review the change-in-use rules before editing depreciation

Treasury Regulation 1.168(i)-4 provides rules for changes in use of MACRS property. The applicable calculation depends on the change and prior treatment. Do not simply restart every asset or apply one new recovery period to the entire report.

Distinguish a properly reported use change from correcting an earlier impermissible depreciation method. Those situations may require different procedures. Ask the preparer whether any correction, election or accounting-method work is needed; this guide does not determine that Form 3115 is always required or never required.

Keep component history intact

A sofa moved to another rental, an appliance retained for the new tenant and an improvement installed during the transition have different histories. Preserve original identifiers and cost records. Do not recreate basis from current retail values. Use the furnishings register and the property timeline guide.

Have the preparer review losses year by year

The new rental arrangement can change the activity analysis. Review the earlier conclusions and current facts rather than automatically reversing prior deductions or assuming future deductions remain usable against wages. Keep any suspended-loss schedule connected to the relevant activity and year. Review the wage-income questions before relying on the prior model.

Do not let an assumed catch-up deduction drive the operating decision

If a study is being considered during the transition, supply all prior schedules and use records. Ask for a written comparison of the relevant implementation options, fees and expected timing. A report ordered before conversion does not guarantee a particular loss character or dollar benefit.

Review LTR study scope, study economics and the CPA handoff. Make the operating choice using the business facts and adviser-reviewed tax consequences together.
