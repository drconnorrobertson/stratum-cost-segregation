Cost segregation for an Airbnb property starts with the owner’s supported investment in the property. A study separates and documents component costs for depreciation analysis; the owner’s preparer decides how the report affects the return. The booking platform does not determine the building classification, eligible basis or ability to use a resulting loss.

Four separate decisions behind an Airbnb study

  1. Property: what was acquired, what transferred with the home and what was added later?
  2. Classification: which component conclusions are supported by actual use, installation and cost evidence?
  3. Implementation: how do the study and prior depreciation records connect?
  4. Economics: when can any additional deduction be used, and what changes on a later sale?

Keep those questions separate in a proposal discussion. A preliminary reclassification percentage does not answer all four. There is no standard percentage of every Airbnb purchase that a study must reclassify.

Purchased furnishings need their own reconciliation

Use the seller’s inventory and purchase agreement to identify what actually transferred. Keep post-closing furniture invoices in a different group, and identify anything already on your depreciation schedule. A design budget, retail replacement quote and acquisition cost are different records. Ask the study team how the report avoids including the same item twice.

For a condo, include the unit boundaries and association records. An amenity pictured on the listing may be association-owned rather than part of your study. For a cabin, document additions, detached spaces and outdoor installations instead of assigning them a category from the listing description. Review the condo boundary guide and furnishing records.

Short stays and material participation require a separate review

The rental-activity exception for average customer use of seven days or less is one part of the analysis. It does not, by itself, establish material participation or make a study deduction usable against W-2 income. Have your preparer evaluate the year’s actual activity, work and loss limitations. Review the comparative participation test before relying on a headline hour target.

Keep a factual calendar of rental periods, personal use and changes in availability. Give the preparer manager and contractor information rather than assuming an owner’s total hours establishes the outcome.

The report date does not reset your property’s tax history

A new report on an older Airbnb does not turn it into a newly acquired property. Supply the original acquisition and service dates, later asset purchases and prior schedules. The preparer determines applicable bonus-depreciation treatment and the implementation procedure. A Form 3115 discussion should identify the relevant facts and responsibility, not promise that every earlier-year deduction can simply be recovered.

Evaluate the fee without a guaranteed-savings example

Stratum studies cost $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Compare that fee and separate implementation costs with the additional deductions the preparer expects you can use. Model future deductions and sale consequences as well. Use the fee-versus-benefit worksheet with your own adviser-supported assumptions.
