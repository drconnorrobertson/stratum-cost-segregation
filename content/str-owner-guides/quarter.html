The MACRS convention helps determine depreciation timing. A cost segregation study may identify assets that require a mid-quarter review, but a closing date or report date alone does not establish the answer.

The more-than-40% test

Publication 946 describes the mid-quarter test by comparing the relevant depreciable bases placed in service during the last three months of the tax year with the relevant total for the year. More than 40% triggers the convention for applicable property. Exactly 40% is not more than 40%.

Apply the required exclusions and basis rules before dividing. The publication excludes residential rental and nonresidential real property, railroad grading or tunnel bores, property placed in service and disposed of in the same year, and property depreciated under a method other than MACRS. Review section 179 treatment and the special depreciation allowance as directed by the applicable instructions rather than substituting purchase price for the test basis.

An example that exposes a common mistake

Assume a calendar-year taxpayer has $50,000 of relevant test basis placed in service in June and $50,000 in November, after all required adjustments and exclusions. The last-three-month portion is $50,000 out of $100,000, or 50%. Two equal acquisitions do not keep the taxpayer below 40%. This illustrates the ratio only, not the classification or eligibility of a particular property.

Collect all applicable additions

The preparer needs the relevant taxpayer's additions beyond the single property in a study. Furnishings for another Airbnb or assets in another business may affect the review. Entity ownership and return reporting determine which records must be brought together; do not combine every property the owner knows about without checking the taxpayer.

  • List each asset, owner, supported basis and service date.
  • Separate building basis from other classifications.
  • Identify same-year dispositions and applicable exclusions.
  • Reconcile elections and allowances with the preparer's test.
  • Save the test with the final depreciation schedules.

Service date is a factual milestone

Property generally enters service when ready and available for its intended use. Delivery, closing, payment and first guest booking can be different dates. A phased renovation can involve several service dates. See the STR records checklist and furnishing guide.

Bonus and state treatment are separate inputs

Eligible bonus treatment may reduce the remaining federal basis depreciated under the convention, but it does not justify skipping the review. Elections, ineligible assets and state differences can matter. Connect the bonus eligibility review with the state schedule checklist. Do not change a service date to obtain a preferred result.
