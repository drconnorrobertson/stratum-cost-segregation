# Cost Segregation for Co-Owned STRs and Partnerships

When two or more people own an Airbnb, a cost segregation study does not decide how they may divide the resulting tax benefits. The property costs, reporting structure, allocation rules and each owner's ability to use a deduction all require review. A cash contribution, ownership percentage or informal agreement does not answer every part of that analysis.

This guide explains the handoff for friends, relatives and partners evaluating a study together. It also addresses why a large property-level depreciation estimate should not be marketed as an equal refund for every owner.

The first useful step is to identify the actual arrangement. Direct co-ownership, a partnership and an LLC with a particular federal tax classification may require different reporting. Do not let the study provider infer that structure from the name on a booking account.

## Is shared ownership automatically a partnership?

Not every jointly owned property arrangement is treated identically. IRS Publication 541 distinguishes certain co-ownership arrangements from organizations classified as partnerships. Services and the activity's facts can matter. A short-stay business should not adopt a classification merely because another rental owner used it.

Give the preparer the ownership documents, agreements and operating facts. Explain who provides services, who contracts with guests and managers, and how receipts and expenses are handled. Ask the preparer to identify the reporting structure before the provider finalizes the report context.

The [LLC ownership FAQ](/blog/do-you-need-llc-airbnb-cost-segregation/) explains why an entity name alone is insufficient. The study should follow the approved ownership and tax history rather than create it.

## Separate the property's basis from an owner's interest

The property's depreciable basis and an owner's basis in a partnership interest are different concepts. Confusing them can produce an attractive property-level estimate that does not translate into a usable deduction for a particular person.

Prepare acquisition and improvement records for the property. Separately, provide the preparer with the ownership, contribution, distribution and relevant liability history needed for the owner-level review. Do not assume that a current account balance or a percentage of the property's market value establishes either calculation.

A transfer of an ownership interest is also not automatically a new cash purchase of every underlying asset. If an owner bought in later, inherited an interest or changed the arrangement, ask about the relevant basis consequences before assigning a new value to the building.

## The study does not authorize a special allocation

In a partnership context, allocation rules and the agreement require professional review. Owners cannot simply assign the largest depreciation amount to the person with the highest tax rate because everyone prefers that outcome.

The applicable rules can involve the partners' economic arrangement, capital accounts, contributed property and other facts. A generic operating-agreement clause is not proof that a specific proposed allocation will be respected. Nor is a cost segregation report a legal opinion on the agreement.

Before commissioning a study around a promised allocation, ask the preparer and relevant advisers to document how the allocation will work and what must be reviewed. Avoid amending an agreement based on a blog's abbreviated description of technical requirements.

## A two-owner illustration

Assume two hypothetical owners hold interests in an STR arrangement. One contributed more cash, one performs more operating work, and both expect a cost segregation study to produce depreciation. Those facts do not establish how every deduction is allocated or used.

The property analysis first needs supported costs and classifications. The reporting analysis then needs the applicable ownership and allocation treatment. Each owner may have different basis, at-risk, participation, income and carryover facts.

The result could differ between owners even when the property-level depreciation total is agreed. The useful projection shows the allocated amount, the limitations applied and the expected timing for each taxpayer. It does not multiply one total by both owners' tax brackets and add the answers together.

This example deliberately contains no deduction percentage or refund amount. Its purpose is to show the sequence and avoid double-counting a shared estimate.

## Evaluate each owner's loss limits

An allocated deduction is not automatically a usable deduction. The preparer may need to apply basis, at-risk, passive activity and other applicable limits, including excess business loss rules where relevant. These reviews are not replaced by the property study.

Do not assume all owners have the same participation conclusion. One may manage operations while another primarily reviews the investment. Rules for a particular interest and activity need to be considered on their facts; an owner's title alone should not be treated as a universal answer.

Read the [passive loss guide](/blog/passive-activity-loss-rules-cost-segregation/), [participation review](/blog/str-100-hour-material-participation-test/) and [excess business loss guide](/blog/excess-business-loss-limitation-cost-segregation/) as separate parts of the discussion. A partner-level carryover should not be presented as current cash savings.

## Reconcile purchases made by different people

Shared rentals often have messy furnishing records. One owner pays for appliances, another buys furniture, and the manager deducts project costs from distributions. The preparer needs to determine how those transactions were recorded and who owns the relevant assets.

Create a source register with the item, payer, property, invoice, reimbursement and prior schedule reference. Do not include an owner-paid invoice again as a separate company purchase if the same amount was already reimbursed and recorded.

The [furnishing guide](/blog/str-furniture-ffe-depreciation/) and [document checklist](/blog/str-cost-segregation-document-checklist/) help organize the evidence. The goal is one coherent cost history, not a separate incompatible asset list from each owner.

## What if someone joins or leaves after the study?

Notify the preparer before changing the ownership schedule. Transfers can raise separate questions about the interest transferred, basis, elections and reporting. A partner-specific adjustment, where applicable, should not be confused with a new basis for all partners.

Do not assume that every transfer creates an upward adjustment or that an election is always the only circumstance in which an adjustment can arise. The relevant partnership rules need to be applied to the actual transaction. A study can provide property detail without determining the entire transfer analysis.

Preserve the original report and subsequent ledger changes. If the property later sells, the adviser needs the history rather than just the most recent ownership percentages.

## Co-owned Airbnb FAQ

### Can only one owner order the study?

One person can coordinate the project, but confirm authority, engagement terms and the reporting context. The provider needs to know which property and taxpayer arrangement the report serves, not merely who submits the inquiry.

### Can the deduction go to the owner who paid the study fee?

Paying a professional fee does not by itself determine the allocation of the property's depreciation. Keep the fee treatment separate from the applicable ownership and allocation rules.

### Does a 50/50 deed mean identical tax savings?

Not automatically. Even where an allocation follows an equal economic arrangement, owner-level facts can affect usability. Ask for separate owner projections rather than assuming identical returns.

### Can cash distributions absorb a suspended loss?

Do not treat a distribution as automatically taxable income available for that purpose. The preparer needs to distinguish distributions, allocated income, basis effects and applicable loss categories.

### Do all owners have to materially participate?

Participation is a taxpayer and activity question. Do not infer a shared conclusion from one owner's work. Ask the preparer what facts and rules apply to each interest and which evidence is needed.

### Can we use an existing report after an ownership change?

The report may remain useful property evidence, but the new transaction and reporting need review. Confirm whether additional analysis or schedules are required instead of changing the name on the cover and assuming implementation is complete.

## A pre-study meeting agenda

Identify the property, owners, reporting arrangement and decision being considered. Confirm the accepted basis history and the documents available. Ask who reviews allocations, who evaluates each owner's limitations and who implements the report.

Request a written list of unresolved issues before relying on an estimate. If a proposed allocation is central to the economics, resolve that question before presenting the study as a guaranteed result. Use the [report handoff guide](/blog/how-to-read-cost-segregation-report/) to connect property evidence with the preparer's work.

Stratum studies cost $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Confirm any separate ownership, partnership advisory or filing work when comparing the full engagement cost. [Discuss the property and study scope](/cost-seg-discovery/) using the actual ownership map and project records.

## Keep a common version of the final records

Agree which report, basis reconciliation and asset schedule are final. Each owner should not independently send a different revision to a different preparer without explaining the change. Identify the person who coordinates corrections and retains the supporting source documents.

When an assumption changes, record the reason and the affected schedules. A new ownership percentage, reimbursement or improvement may require different follow-up from a simple address correction. Version control does not determine the tax result, but it helps prevent inconsistent inputs from being treated as though they describe the same transaction.
