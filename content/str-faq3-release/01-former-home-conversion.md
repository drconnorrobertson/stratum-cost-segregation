# Turning Your Former Home Into an Airbnb: Basis and Depreciation FAQ

Turning a former home into an Airbnb does not automatically give the property a new purchase price for depreciation. Before a cost segregation study divides costs among components, the preparer needs to establish the rental conversion basis, the relevant use history and when the property became ready and available for its intended rental use.

That is a different starting point from buying a rental in a current cash transaction. An owner may have an old closing statement, years of improvements, a new appraisal and a mortgage balance that all show different numbers. None should be substituted for the required tax analysis merely because it is the most convenient figure.

This guide explains how to build the conversion file and connect it to a study without assuming that appreciation, refinancing or an Airbnb listing creates a fresh depreciation opportunity on the property's full current value.

## What basis starts the depreciation review?

IRS Publication 551 explains that when personal-use property changes to business or rental use, the depreciation basis is the lower of fair market value at the change or adjusted basis at that time. Land is not depreciable and must be addressed in the allocation.

The preparer needs both sides of the comparison. A current appraisal may help establish value, but it does not by itself establish adjusted basis. An original purchase price may help reconstruct cost, but it does not establish the property's value at conversion or every intervening adjustment.

Keep the comparison and supporting records with the study file. The provider should work from the accepted cost pool rather than treat a new appraisal as though the owner just bought the house again.

## An example with clearly labeled inputs

Assume a hypothetical preparer-supported analysis establishes $400,000 of adjusted basis and $360,000 of conversion-date fair market value for the depreciable house portion, with land separately excluded. The lower amount is $360,000 for this simplified depreciation-basis comparison.

If those inputs were reversed, the lower amount would still control the comparison. Appreciation does not, by itself, permit the owner to replace adjusted basis with the higher market value. A cost segregation allocation should not undo that starting rule by assigning component costs that exceed the accepted pool.

This example does not calculate depreciation, bonus eligibility, a loss or sale treatment. It illustrates why the inputs must be defined consistently. Comparing a house-only adjusted basis with a value that includes land would not be a meaningful like-for-like reconciliation.

## Reconstruct the adjusted-basis history

Begin with the original acquisition records and relevant later events. Give the preparer improvement invoices, credits, reimbursements and other history they identify as relevant. Avoid combining every payment made during ownership into a single “investment in home” number.

A mortgage balance is not the basis schedule. Refinancing proceeds are not automatically an increase in property basis. Spending those proceeds on an improvement creates a separate event that needs its own evidence and treatment.

Use the [land allocation guide](/blog/land-value-allocation-cost-segregation/) and [refinancing guide](/blog/cost-segregation-after-refinance/) for those related questions. The final reconciliation should explain the accepted starting amount, adjustments and exclusions rather than presenting an unexplained total.

## Establish a supportable conversion timeline

Moving out, completing renovations, publishing a listing and welcoming the first guest can happen on different dates. Publication 527 discusses rental service in terms of readiness and availability for the specific use. A first-booking timestamp should not be asked to establish every asset's history.

Keep completion records, photographs, communications and availability information that explain the actual transition. Record unresolved work or restrictions rather than selecting a convenient year-end date. If the property entered service in stages, identify the affected assets and the separate events.

The [phased-opening guide](/blog/str-renovation-phased-opening/) explains how to organize this evidence. The study date itself does not reset the original acquisition date or establish that the home was available for rent earlier.

## Keep existing furnishings separate from new purchases

A converted home may contain furniture the owner used personally, new items acquired for guests and equipment already recorded elsewhere. Those groups should not be treated as a single new purchase simply because they now appear in the same listing.

Prepare an inventory identifying the item, acquisition history, supported cost, relevant value information, prior use and current location. Give the preparer the facts needed to determine the appropriate basis and treatment for converted personal property. A retail replacement estimate is not automatically the amount the owner paid or the accepted tax basis.

Use the [furnishing records guide](/blog/str-furniture-ffe-depreciation/) for missing receipts, moved assets and replacements. Keep new renovation invoices distinct from the conversion of the pre-existing house and contents.

## Does converting the home guarantee bonus depreciation?

No. The preparer must review the applicable asset categories, acquisition history, service dates, elections and other eligibility requirements. Converting a property to rental use does not automatically turn every asset into a current-year acquisition.

Ask the provider to identify which assumptions support any estimate. Then ask the preparer how the accepted classifications and dates affect the return. A study's allocation is not a conclusion that all components qualify for immediate expensing.

The [bonus depreciation guide](/blog/bonus-depreciation-2026-rental-property/) should be read with the historical records. Avoid applying today's percentage to an entire converted-home value without the asset-specific review.

## What if you still use the home personally?

Keep an accurate use calendar and explain any owner-only areas. A converted home does not necessarily become exclusively rental property just because it has an active listing. Owner stays, family use and partly rented space can require additional allocation and deduction review.

The [vacation-rental guide](/blog/cost-segregation-vacation-rentals/) addresses that separate issue. Material participation and personal-use rules should not be collapsed into one conclusion. The preparer needs the actual use facts before projecting how much of the study's depreciation may be usable.

If only part of the home is offered to guests, describe the spaces, access and shared areas. The study should not infer rental use of the entire parcel from one booking account.

## Former-home conversion FAQ

### Can I depreciate the home's new appraisal value?

Not automatically. The depreciation-basis comparison requires adjusted basis and conversion-date fair market value under the applicable rules. Provide both records and the land allocation rather than treating the appraisal as a new purchase price.

### Does moving the home into an LLC reset basis?

Do not assume that an entity or title change creates a new cost basis. The transaction and reporting need their own review. See the [LLC ownership FAQ](/blog/do-you-need-llc-airbnb-cost-segregation/) before changing the study's owner or acquisition inputs.

### Can I include renovations completed before renting?

Give the preparer the actual scope, dates and invoices. Acquisition history, capitalization, readiness and later improvements need to be reconciled. Do not classify all pre-opening work from the invoice heading alone.

### What if I cannot find old improvement invoices?

Identify the gaps and collect available supporting records. Separate confirmed amounts from estimates and unresolved questions. A study does not remove the need for support or authorize an owner to invent historical spending.

### Is the depreciation basis also the basis used for every later sale calculation?

Not necessarily. Publication 551 explains separate considerations for later gain and loss calculations on converted property. Retain both the original basis history and the conversion analysis instead of discarding one after the study.

### Can I get a study after already filing a rental return?

A later study may be evaluated, but the preparer must determine the available implementation procedure. The [Form 3115 guide](/blog/form-3115-look-back-cost-segregation/) explains why the report alone does not establish a current catch-up deduction.

## Close the basis question before relying on savings

Ask the preparer to approve a clear reconciliation and identify unresolved use or procedural questions. Give the provider the accepted version, together with the asset and improvement history. Keep a record of revisions so the report does not rely on a superseded cost pool.

Then evaluate the amount expected to be usable, implementation fees, state treatment and likely holding period. A larger appraisal or a successful listing does not itself establish that a study is worthwhile. Use the [ROI checklist](/blog/roi-cost-segregation-study/) with realistic assumptions.

Stratum studies cost $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Bring the conversion timeline and basis records to a [discovery call](/cost-seg-discovery/), and confirm separate tax advisory and filing responsibilities.

## Keep the conversion decision separate from later changes

Once the preparer accepts the initial conversion analysis, preserve that version with its supporting documents. Later improvements, furnishing replacements or changes between rental types should be recorded as later events rather than silently changing the original conversion facts.

If a revised valuation or previously missing invoice changes the analysis, document the reason and ask the preparer about the consequences for any filed returns. The provider should know which version is current, but the study report should not decide the filing procedure simply by printing a revised number.

This record is especially useful when an owner changes managers or preparers. A one-page chronology identifying the original purchase, conversion, accepted basis and subsequent projects gives the next reviewer a clear starting point. Keep the underlying documents available; the chronology is an index, not a replacement for evidence.
