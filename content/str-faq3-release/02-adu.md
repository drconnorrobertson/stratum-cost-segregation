# ADU Cost Segregation: Airbnb, Shared Costs and Depreciation FAQ

An accessory dwelling unit can create several tax and recordkeeping questions before a cost segregation study begins. The owner needs to identify the relevant costs, distinguish the rental unit from personal-use property, document shared work and establish the applicable use and service history. The label “ADU” does not automatically determine an asset class, a building recovery period or a usable rental loss.

This guide covers detached cottages, attached units and converted garages without assigning each a promised reclassification percentage. A useful analysis starts with what was acquired or built and how it is used. It does not assume that a compact floor plan guarantees better tax results.

For an ADU used as an Airbnb, keep the property study separate from the preparer's activity and participation analysis. Short stays and personal involvement do not, by themselves, establish that the building belongs on a particular depreciation schedule.

## Is an Airbnb ADU automatically nonresidential property?

Do not use the short-stay rental exceptions under the passive activity rules as a substitute for the depreciation classification review. The building's use and applicable definitions must be evaluated separately. Material participation does not itself select a 27.5-year or 39-year recovery period.

Give the preparer the unit configuration, actual rental arrangements, services and owner-use facts. Explain whether the ADU operates independently or shares facilities and access with the main home. The provider should use the preparer-approved context rather than infer treatment from the marketing description.

The [STR strategy guide](/blog/cost-segregation-str-tax-loophole/) and [classification examples](/blog/5-year-7-year-15-year-property-examples/) explain why activity treatment and component classifications are different questions.

## Start with the type of project

A newly built detached unit, an addition and a conversion of existing space have different histories. For new construction, gather the contract, invoices, change orders and completion records. For a conversion, also preserve the original property's basis and use history.

Do not treat an existing garage as though it was purchased again at its current market value when converted. Do not assume the entire main-home basis belongs in the ADU study. The preparer needs a supported allocation and an explanation of which property changed use or was improved.

Use the [former-home conversion basis guide](/resources/scenarios/personal-residence-converted-to-rental-basis-review/) when existing personal-use property enters the rental operation. Keep conversion of old property separate from new spending on the project.

## Allocate shared costs with evidence

An ADU project can include work serving the unit, the main home and common areas. A new utility connection, driveway project or landscape invoice may have a broader scope than the rental unit alone. The study should not assume that every cost in the contractor's file belongs entirely to the ADU.

Ask the contractor to identify quantities, locations and the functions served. Provide plans and photographs that explain shared systems. The preparer and provider can then review an allocation grounded in the project rather than choosing a convenient percentage without support.

The [contractor invoice guide](/blog/str-contractor-invoice-breakdown/) helps organize the evidence. Retain the source documents and the accepted allocation method so later questions can be resolved without rebuilding the project history.

## An example of a mixed-scope invoice

Assume a fictional owner receives a $30,000 invoice covering work on an ADU and the surrounding parcel. Available detail supports $12,000 of work serving the unit, $8,000 serving the main home and $10,000 requiring further allocation. The three groups total $30,000.

The unresolved $10,000 should not automatically become rental basis. The owner needs additional scope or another supportable approach for review. The example also does not determine whether any accepted cost is a repair, an improvement or a particular recovery class.

This is a practical reason to resolve shared scope before requesting a precise savings estimate. If the provider receives $30,000 while the preparer accepts only a supported portion for the rental review, the report and return can begin from incompatible numbers.

## Do kitchens, bathrooms and utility lines guarantee short-life treatment?

No. A unit needs ordinary building systems to function, and those systems are not automatically personal property merely because they serve an ADU. Installation, function, ownership and the applicable asset rules matter.

Publication 527 provides recovery-period examples for residential rental property, including certain furniture and appliances, while Publication 946 explains the broader depreciation framework. Neither should be replaced by a blanket claim that all cabinetry, flooring, plumbing or dedicated electrical work qualifies for five-year treatment.

The [component evidence guide](/blog/components-reclassified-cost-segregation/) and [outdoor improvement review](/blog/land-improvements-15-year-property-cost-segregation/) help identify the information needed. A study should explain its classifications rather than rely on an advertised “typical ADU percentage.”

## Keep furniture and owner use visible

An ADU furnished from the owner's household may contain old personally used items, new purchases and manager-supplied property. Keep those groups separate. The current inventory establishes what is present; it does not establish every item's cost, ownership or tax basis.

Also document any personal stays, family use and owner-only space. The rental unit's relationship to the main home does not eliminate the need for a use review. Guest availability, actual rentals and personal use should be recorded for their relevant purposes rather than combined into one unexplained occupancy total.

Read the [furnishing guide](/blog/str-furniture-ffe-depreciation/) and [vacation-rental use review](/blog/cost-segregation-vacation-rentals/) before treating the entire project as exclusively rental property.

## What dates matter when the ADU opens?

The contractor's payment date, project completion, readiness for rent and first guest booking can differ. A later patio, furnishing purchase or utility project may require its own service-date review. Preserve those events instead of assigning every asset the same date by default.

If the main house was already operating as a rental, the ADU's later opening does not automatically restart the entire property's depreciation. Likewise, an existing home's date should not be copied onto new ADU construction without analysis.

The [phased-opening guide](/blog/str-renovation-phased-opening/) provides an event-ledger approach. Ask the preparer to resolve the dates before the report is implemented.

## ADU cost segregation FAQ

### Is a detached ADU always a better study candidate than a garage conversion?

Not automatically. Costs, evidence, use, applicable treatment and usable benefits determine the decision. A detached structure may create different records and classifications, but that does not establish a guaranteed return.

### Can I depreciate the land under the ADU?

Land is not depreciable. The relevant cost and allocation review should distinguish land from the building and any depreciable improvements. Do not move land value into short-life property because the new unit occupies part of the parcel.

### Does a building permit establish the depreciation start date?

It is one record in the project history, not necessarily the complete service-date conclusion. Explain what the permit or approval establishes and retain completion and availability evidence as well.

### Does short-term rental use guarantee that losses offset wages?

No. The activity, participation and applicable loss limits require separate review. A component study does not establish that the owner can use the entire resulting depreciation against employment income.

### Should an ADU be priced as a separate property study?

Confirm the engagement's scope and property definition in writing. Do not assume the advertised per-square-foot rate resolves every question about a main home, ADU and shared work. Identify what is included before comparing proposals.

### Are all ADU projects above a particular cost automatically worthwhile?

No universal construction threshold establishes positive ROI. A supported usable-benefit estimate, full fees, implementation costs and holding-period review are more useful than an automatic rule based on project size.

## Prepare one coordinated project file

Bring the original property history, approved basis allocation, construction records, shared-cost explanation, furnishing inventory and use timeline together. Mark missing evidence and assign responsibility for resolving it. A well-organized file should make clear what belongs to the rental unit and what remains personal or shared.

Stratum studies cost $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Confirm the scope and any separately engaged filing or advisory work. Use the [proposal checklist](/blog/str-cost-segregation-proposal-comparison/) and [discovery call](/cost-seg-discovery/) to discuss the actual project rather than a generic promised deduction.

## Review the proposal against the actual project boundary

A proposal should identify whether the review covers only the ADU, the wider rental property or a specified construction project. If the main home remains personal-use property, make that fact visible before the provider estimates the cost pool. If shared work is included, identify how the allocation will be reviewed and who approves it.

Ask which records the provider expects from the owner and contractor. An estimate based on a complete invoice file can involve different work from reconstructing costs with substantial gaps. Delivery timing should reflect those dependencies rather than an unsupported promise that every project follows the same schedule.

Before accepting the final report, compare the property boundary, accepted costs and service dates with the preparer's records. Resolve differences while the project team is available. A small unit can still have a complicated history, and square footage alone does not establish the simplicity of its tax implementation.
