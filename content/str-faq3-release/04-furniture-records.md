# STR Furniture Depreciation: Missing Receipts and Owner FAQs

Missing furniture receipts create an evidence problem, not permission to use an arbitrary furnishing percentage. An Airbnb owner should identify what is owned, how it was acquired, its supported cost or relevant basis, prior treatment and when it became available for rental use. A current room photograph can show an item exists without establishing all of those facts.

The right response is to organize the available records and make the gaps visible. A cost segregation study may use appropriate evidence and disclosed estimation methods within its scope, but the report does not eliminate the need to support the amounts used on a return.

This guide explains how to assemble an STR furnishing file, preserve the distinction between purchases and transferred items, and avoid duplicate deductions while resolving missing documentation with the provider and preparer.

## Start with three acquisition groups

First, identify furnishings transferred with the property. Keep the purchase agreement, seller inventory and any supported allocation. The seller's original retail receipt is not automatically the amount the buyer paid for a used item within a combined transaction.

Second, identify purchases made after acquisition. Gather final invoices, credits, payment records, delivery and installation information. A deposit and the final invoice should not be counted as two separate purchases.

Third, identify existing or moved items. Preserve the original acquisition history, prior use, previous depreciation and transfer records. Moving an item to another property does not create a new purchase cost. Converting personal property to rental use also requires a basis review rather than automatic use of a current retail price.

## What can a bank or card statement establish?

A payment record can help show that money was paid to a vendor on a date. It may not identify the items, credits, property location or installation details needed for the asset schedule. Treat it as part of the evidence package rather than a complete substitute for every missing fact.

Look for vendor order histories, emailed invoices, delivery records and warranty or product records that can be connected to the payment. Keep the sources together with a short explanation of what each establishes and what remains unknown.

IRS Publication 583 discusses supporting records and asset information needed for depreciation and later dispositions. A tidy spreadsheet should connect to those records; it should not become the sole source of a historical number that nobody can explain.

## Reconstruct records without inventing precision

Create a register listing each material item or supported group, location, owner, acquisition event and available source references. Add columns for uncertainty and the professional responsible for resolving it. Separate confirmed facts from estimates or unresolved assumptions.

A provider should explain any estimation method used and its limits. A photograph, cost database or replacement quote may answer a different question from an original purchase invoice. Do not treat all valuation or costing evidence as interchangeable.

The [document checklist](/blog/str-cost-segregation-document-checklist/) includes a blank records register, and a [furnishing evidence worksheet](/assets/downloads/str-furnishing-evidence.csv) can help organize the item-level handoff. Neither worksheet assigns basis, a recovery class or a deduction.

## A reconciliation example

Assume a fictional owner has $8,000 of vendor invoices, a $1,000 deposit that is already included in those final invoices, and $600 of documented returns. Ignoring other adjustments, the invoice amount after the returns is $7,400. Adding the deposit again would duplicate part of the spending.

The example does not establish how the purchases should be classified or expensed. It illustrates why payment totals and asset costs need reconciliation. If the invoices include several properties or manager-owned items, those facts require further review before the accepted amount enters a particular property's schedule.

Preserve the source documents and the explanation of the reconciliation. A later preparer should be able to understand why the total differs from the sum of every bank-account outflow labeled “furniture.”

## Furniture is not automatically seven-year property

Publication 527 lists furniture, certain appliances and carpeting used in residential rental property in its five-year GDS examples. It separately lists office furniture and equipment as seven-year property. Confirm the relevant activity, asset class and depreciation system rather than assigning every sofa or table the same period from a generic FF&E label.

That component review does not automatically determine the building's recovery period. Nor does a short recovery period establish bonus eligibility, personal-use treatment or whether the owner can currently use a resulting loss.

The [classification examples](/blog/5-year-7-year-15-year-property-examples/) and [bonus depreciation guide](/blog/bonus-depreciation-2026-rental-property/) explain those separate questions. Keep the source of the cost distinct from the authority supporting its treatment.

## Describe installed items before choosing a label

Smart-home devices, wiring, lighting, cabinets and outdoor amenities can involve different installation and function facts. A room inventory alone may not explain the relevant property. Provide photographs, scope and invoices where the classification depends on more than an item name.

For a hot tub, distinguish the unit, installation, connections and surrounding work. For equipment supplied by a manager, clarify ownership. For a replacement, identify the old item and its prior schedule as well as the new purchase.

The [hot tub and pool guide](/blog/airbnb-hot-tub-pool-depreciation/) and [component evidence review](/blog/components-reclassified-cost-segregation/) help frame these questions. Avoid treating every object visible in guest photographs as a separately owned short-life asset.

## Can an expense election replace the records review?

No. The de minimis safe harbor and other expensing provisions have requirements beyond an item appearing inexpensive. The preparer needs the applicable accounting policy, book treatment, election and asset facts. An election does not make duplicated spending or unsupported ownership correct.

For the de minimis safe harbor, the IRS guidance distinguishes taxpayers with and without an applicable financial statement. Do not assume every taxpayer needs a written policy or that no beginning-of-year procedure matters. Review the current requirements for the actual taxpayer.

Use the [safe-harbor comparison guide](/blog/de-minimis-safe-harbor-vs-cost-segregation/) and [repairs-versus-improvements FAQ](/blog/airbnb-repairs-improvements-cost-segregation/) before forcing all spending into the study's capital-cost pool.

## Missing furniture records FAQ

### Can I use today's replacement price for old furniture?

Not automatically. Identify what the number represents and why it is relevant. A current retail quote is not proof of the owner's original cost, converted-property basis or amount paid in a furnished acquisition.

### Is a seller's inventory enough by itself?

It can establish useful item information, but the acquisition allocation still needs support. Reconcile it to the purchase agreement and approved cost pool. See the [seller furniture guide](/blog/str-seller-furniture-allocation/) for that separate event.

### What if the manager bought the items?

Determine who owns them, how the costs were paid or reimbursed and how they were recorded. A charge on a manager statement does not answer every ownership or classification question.

### Can I move furniture between Airbnbs and depreciate it again?

Moving an asset does not create another purchase. Preserve its existing history and have the preparer address any relevant changes in use or reporting. Keep the location record current without duplicating basis.

### What if an old item was thrown away?

Record the removal and connect it to the prior asset schedule. The preparer must determine the disposition treatment; do not assume a current replacement invoice equals the old item's remaining basis.

### Does a five-year class mean a full current deduction?

No. Recovery period, method, conventions, bonus or expense elections and owner-level limits are separate inputs. Do not infer the final return outcome from a single class label.

### Should the study and my preparer both include the furniture?

Agree on responsibility and matching identifiers. The report and tax ledger should reconcile, not each add the same purchase as a separate asset. Mark excluded items and unresolved questions in the final handoff.

## Preserve a usable record after implementation

Retain the final inventory, source references, accepted cost or basis and schedules actually used. Keep credits, replacements and transfers connected to the original identifier so a later review does not begin with an unexplained list of room contents.

A missing-receipt question should be resolved before a precise savings estimate is treated as final. Use the [report handoff guide](/blog/how-to-read-cost-segregation-report/) to identify which evidence is accepted and who will address remaining issues.

Stratum studies cost $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Confirm which furnishings are included and any separately engaged advisory or filing work. [Discuss the property and scope](/cost-seg-discovery/) using the records available rather than a guessed percentage of the listing value.

## Resolve exceptions before the final inventory is accepted

Review missing or conflicting records as a separate list. Identify the item, the question, the sources already checked and the person responsible for the next step. A provider can then explain whether the gap changes scope, requires additional evidence or remains an explicit limitation.

Do not remove uncertainty from the spreadsheet just because the report is approaching completion. If a number remains estimated, retain the explanation and the preparer's conclusion about its use. If an item is excluded, preserve that decision so it is not inadvertently added again later.

The final inventory should be usable by someone who did not attend the original calls. Matching identifiers, source references and clear exception notes make that possible without relying on memory or repeating the entire investigation during a future sale or replacement.
