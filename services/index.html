What to request in the written deliverables

Ask for a proposal you and your preparer can evaluate. It should identify the property and periods, the inspection or evidence method, the assigned analyst and reviewer, and the report format. Record any assumptions about missing plans, invoices or access so those limitations do not disappear between the estimate and the final report.

  • Basis reconciliation: a bridge from the supported acquisition or project cost to the assets analyzed, with land and any excluded amounts identified.
  • Component schedules: costs, classifications and supporting explanations that can be traced back to the property records.
  • Separate asset histories: a clear distinction between acquired assets, later improvements and furnishings already recorded separately.
  • Review questions: a process for resolving discrepancies or preparer requests before implementation.
  • Completion record: the delivered report version, remaining questions and the person responsible for each next action.

Keep the study fee and tax implementation scope clear

Review current Stratum study pricing, then confirm the applicable fee and included work in writing. Ask whether additional inspections, revised records, report corrections, tax forms, amended returns or examination representation are included or separately scoped. Do not infer a delivery deadline or unlimited support from a brief estimate.

For an existing property, the return preparer determines the appropriate correction approach. A look-back study does not automatically mean Form 3115 is required or available. The IRS Form 3115 instructions describe accounting-method change procedures and requirements; the applicable facts and current procedures need review.

Ask your preparer whether the delivered schedules reconcile with the records they will use. Keep unresolved questions visible and agree who will close them.
