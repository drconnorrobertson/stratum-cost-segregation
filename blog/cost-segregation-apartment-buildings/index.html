Apartment Cost Segregation: Components, Costs & Records

Updated October 9, 2026 · Stratum Cost Segregation · Editorial policy

Explore apartment cost segregation, component evidence, renovation records, passive-loss questions, and the information needed for a property-specific study.

What apartment cost segregation actually answers

An apartment study separates supported component costs from the building's remaining basis. It should explain what was examined, which records support the costs, and why the proposed classifications fit the installation. A percentage quoted for another building cannot answer those questions for yours. Residential rental buildings generally use a 27.5-year GDS recovery period; land is not depreciable. Confirm the applicable depreciation system before comparing a study with your current schedule.

Compare components by function, not by their names

Prepare a room-by-room inventory of appliances, floor finishes, window treatments, common-area equipment, and site improvements. Supply photographs and invoices where available. Do not assume that every fixture, cabinet, utility line, pool, or paved surface has the same recovery period. Ask the provider to explain permanent attachment, actual function, ownership, and the distinction between building systems and equipment-specific installations. The IRS audit guide describes how examiners evaluate study methodology and supporting classifications; it is not an approval of a provider.

Separate the acquisition from each renovation

For a value-add property, create a project file for each unit turn and common-area renovation. Record the original component, retained work, removed work, new installation, final invoice, credits, and service date. Reconcile those files to the fixed-asset schedule. Ask your preparer whether removed components, repair-versus-improvement treatment, or a method change require separate action. A renovation budget and the purchase-price allocation are different cost pools.

A useful basis example without a savings promise

Suppose an illustrative purchase price is $6,200,000 and the supported allocation to land is $900,000. Before reviewing other basis adjustments, the remaining amount is $5,300,000. The next step is a supported component analysis, not an assumed reclassification percentage. Ask for a reconciliation showing excluded costs, each classified asset group, and the remaining building amount. This example demonstrates the cost bridge only; it does not calculate first-year depreciation or claim a client result.

Will a deduction reduce your current tax?

Have the preparer review basis, at-risk exposure, passive activity limitations, relevant elections, and state treatment before describing an estimated deduction as cash savings. Rental losses are generally passive, but exceptions and allowances may apply. Real estate professional status and material participation require separate review. A new report also does not change an older asset's acquisition history. Bonus eligibility depends on the applicable dates and property requirements.

What to bring to an apartment study discussion

Bring the executed purchase agreement, closing statement, land allocation support, prior depreciation schedules, unit inventory, renovation ledger, plans, and photographs. Identify who owns the property and which entity paid for each project. Request a written scope, fee, missing-record list, and CPA handoff plan. Compare the study's cost with a preparer-reviewed projection that includes implementation costs and anticipated sale consequences.
