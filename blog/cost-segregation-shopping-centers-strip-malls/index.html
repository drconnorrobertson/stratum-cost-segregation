Retail Center Cost Segregation: Leases, Site Work & Costs

Updated October 9, 2026 · Stratum Cost Segregation · Editorial policy

Prepare a retail-center cost segregation review with lease records, tenant buildouts, site-work evidence, purchase allocations, and a clear CPA handoff.

One retail center can contain several different cost histories

A shopping center may combine an acquired shell, later tenant buildouts, parking work, signs, landscaping, and equipment. Begin by separating those histories. A single reclassification percentage obscures differences between what was purchased, what was installed later, and what belongs to tenants. Ask the study provider to define the analyzed cost pool and reconcile it with the owner's records.

Document site work without assuming one classification

Create a site plan identifying parking areas, sidewalks, drainage, lighting, signs, retaining features, utilities, and landscaping. Record each item's purpose, location, installation, and supported cost. The tax treatment of a site component can depend on its function and relationship to the building. Do not treat every outdoor item as 15-year property or include nondepreciable land in a depreciable cost pool.

Read leases before counting tenant assets

For every unit, record the tenant, lease period, work letter, landlord allowance, buildout scope, and supporting invoices. Distinguish landlord-owned assets from tenant-owned assets and items whose ownership is unresolved. A reimbursement does not by itself settle the tax ownership question. Ask the preparer to resolve allowance treatment and ensure the same work is not counted in two different cost pools.

Keep tenant turnover and replacements visible

When a tenant leaves, compare the prior buildout schedule with demolition plans, invoices, and replacement work. Mark what was removed, retained, or reused. Ask the preparer whether a disposition election or other adjustment is appropriate. A vacant suite does not prove every asset in it was retired. Retain the record supporting the actual removal and the relationship to the existing depreciation schedule.

Build an acquisition-to-report bridge

For an illustrative center with a new parking project, keep the acquisition allocation and the later paving invoice separate. List purchase basis, land allocation, other supported adjustments, excluded assets, and each later addition. Reconcile deposits, credits, and final payments to avoid duplicating the project cost. Ask for unresolved items to remain in an exception list rather than being silently assigned to a preferred recovery period.

Decide whether the engagement fits your plans

Discuss the expected holding period, future tenant turnover, prior studies, current depreciation, and likely filing work with your preparer. Obtain a written scope and fee before commissioning a full report. A preparer-reviewed comparison should address deduction timing, owner-level limitations, state treatment, and sale consequences. Use the component evidence to support the decision rather than treating a general industry percentage as a property-specific result.
