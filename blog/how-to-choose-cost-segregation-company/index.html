How to Choose the Best Cost Segregation Company

Compare cost segregation companies by study evidence, fees, qualifications, and CPA support. Use Stratum’s buyer checklist and provider comparison worksheet.

Updated September 27, 2026 · Published by Stratum Cost Segregation

The best cost segregation company for your property should provide a supported component analysis, a clear scope and fee, and a report your tax preparer can use. If you are comparing cost seg companies, start with the property and the evidence behind the proposal. The largest preliminary deduction is not, by itself, a measure of report quality.

Compare the service you are actually buying

A software-generated report, a property-specific study, and a study bundled with tax-return work are different offerings. A company may sell more than one. Ask which offering the quote covers and how it addresses your property’s complexity. Use the same acquisition documents and improvement facts when requesting proposals so the assumptions can be compared.

Match the provider to the property

For an STR, reconcile furnishings, guest amenities, personal-use periods, and later improvements. Our STR-specific provider guide expands those questions. For a commercial or mixed-use project, ask for experience with comparable construction and the relevant ownership arrangements; the property-type guides outline scoping considerations.

For an older property, bring the existing asset schedule and ask the return preparer how a new study would be implemented. For a recently built property, collect final construction costs and owner-supplied items. Choosing a company before assembling those facts can leave you comparing estimates based on different properties in all but name.

Evaluate the economics with your preparer

A study fee buys an analysis, not a guaranteed tax result. Ask the preparer to consider whether the owner can use deductions currently, the expected holding period, state treatment, and potential consequences on sale. A proposal can be technically detailed yet provide limited immediate benefit for a particular owner. Read when a study may not fit before assuming every property should proceed.

The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide is a useful reference for discussing study methodology and documentation. It is examiner guidance, not an IRS certification of a provider or a promise that a report will be accepted. Publication 946 provides broader depreciation guidance for the preparer’s review.

Questions before hiring

Which cost segregation company is best?

There is no evidence here that establishes one universal winner. The best fit depends on your property, available records, required deliverables, fee, and support needs. Obtain comparable proposals and have the intended return preparer review the scope.

Is the cheapest cost seg report the best value?

Price is one input. A lower fee may cover a different service, while a higher fee does not prove a better result. Compare the actual evidence, review, exclusions, and follow-up obligations.

Does an engineering label guarantee IRS acceptance?

No. Ask how the property is documented, how costs are reconciled, and how classifications are supported. A marketing label cannot guarantee an examination outcome.

Should I choose a local company?

Ask how the provider will gather the property evidence and coordinate with the preparer. A local address alone does not demonstrate the quality of a study, and nationwide service does not remove the need for property-specific documentation.
