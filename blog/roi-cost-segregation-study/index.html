Should I get a cost segregation study?

Updated October 19, 2026 · Stratum Cost Segregation · Published by AE Tax Advisors · Editorial policy

Consider a study when a property-specific estimate shows that the value of deductions you can use, at the time you can use them, justifies the study fee and return implementation costs. A large accelerated deduction alone does not answer the question.

This guide helps you prepare a Stratum study decision with your tax preparer. It is educational and does not predict your deduction, refund or return.

Five checks before commissioning a study

  1. Confirm the property and records. Gather the closing statement, land allocation, placed-in-service date, improvement invoices and current depreciation schedule. Land is not depreciable; your preparer should confirm the basis available for analysis.
  2. Ask which deductions you can use. Passive activity, at-risk and other loss limitations can defer the benefit. A short-term rental label alone does not establish how a loss will be treated.
  3. Compare the complete cost. Stratum pricing is $1 per square foot, with a $2,000 minimum per property. Confirm the written scope and any separate CPA fees for return implementation. See study pricing.
  4. Model your holding period. Review the timing of deductions, expected sale and possible depreciation recapture with your preparer. Accelerating deductions can change their timing and later tax consequences.
  5. Confirm the filing-year treatment. The applicable depreciation rules, acquisition and service dates, elections and prior reporting matter. Ask the preparer to verify them rather than applying a generic bonus depreciation percentage.

Compare usable benefit with the fee

Start with the additional deduction compared with your existing depreciation method, then ask your preparer how much is deductible now and what tax rate applies. Compare the resulting estimated tax reduction with all implementation costs. For a longer-term comparison, model future deductions and sale consequences too.

Illustration, not a forecast: assume a 2,500-square-foot property has a $2,500 Stratum fee, a preparer estimates $20,000 of additional deductions are usable this year, and a 30% combined marginal rate applies. The illustrative current-year tax reduction is $6,000; subtracting only the study fee leaves $3,500 before separate preparation costs and future tax effects. If none of that additional deduction is usable now, the current-year reduction from it is $0. These assumptions do not establish what your property qualifies for.

When to wait or decline

Pause if the records cannot support the basis, the expected usable benefit does not justify the costs, or a near-term sale changes the economics. If losses would be suspended, compare the timing of their potential use before paying for a study. There is no universal property-value threshold that guarantees a worthwhile result.

Questions to take to your CPA

  • How much of the estimated additional deduction can I use this year, and why?
  • What changes if I sell sooner or my income changes?
  • What reporting, elections or accounting-method work is required, and who handles it?
  • What is the estimated benefit after study and implementation fees, including future tax effects?

Prepare a property-specific review

Bring your property address, square footage, purchase and improvement records, depreciation schedule and expected holding period. Compare provider study scopes and review cost segregation versus standard depreciation before deciding.

Primary guidance and limits

  • IRS Publication 946: depreciation and property basis.
  • IRS Publication 925: passive activity and at-risk limitations.
  • IRS Publication 544: dispositions and depreciation recapture.
