Cost Segregation for an STR Portfolio: Keep Properties Separate

Prepare multiple vacation rentals for cost segregation with property identifiers, purchase reconciliations, furniture-transfer records, and a consistent CPA handoff.

Published September 27, 2026 by Stratum Cost Segregation

Use one stable identifier per property

An address, LLC name, booking nickname, and manager reference may all describe the same rental. Choose a property identifier and include it on the document index, invoice allocation, inventory, and study request. Keep an entity-to-property map when more than one taxpayer is involved. A clear identity system prevents records from one home from being used to explain another.

Reconcile each acquisition independently

Each property needs its own transaction documents, land allocation support, improvement history, and existing depreciation schedule. A portfolio acquisition may require additional allocation work; do not divide the price by the number of homes without a supported process. Ask the preparer to approve the property-level basis schedules before the provider prepares individual component analyses.

Track bulk orders and transfers

A single furniture purchase can supply several rentals. Allocate invoice quantities to the actual destinations, reconcile the allocated total to the vendor invoice, and mark any remaining inventory. When an item moves between homes, preserve its history instead of describing it as a new purchase at the receiving property. Ask the bookkeeper how transfers are reflected in the asset records.

Illustration: one delivery, three properties

An owner orders nine beds and sends three to each of three rentals. The invoice appears once in the central payment file and is cross-referenced in each property’s inventory. The three allocations should reconcile to the one purchase total. If delivery or assembly costs need allocation, record the method and have it reviewed rather than quietly rounding each property up.

Scope reports and follow-up questions

A multi-property engagement should state how many property reports or schedules will be delivered, which entities and years are covered, and who handles missing records. Ask whether the quoted fee assumes consistent construction or complete invoices. Similar floor plans can help organize evidence, but they do not prove identical costs, ownership, or service dates.

Maintain a portfolio exception register

List properties with missing closing documents, unclear furnishing allocations, mixed personal use, incomplete renovations, or an expected sale. Resolve the biggest uncertainties before projecting a combined tax result. Keep each study linked to its supporting file and the preparer’s implementation record. Portfolio organization improves reviewability without assuming that every home produces the same outcome.
